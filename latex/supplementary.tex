\section*{Table S4. Ablation --- Zero-Vector vs.\ Shuffled-Feature Comparison}

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Two approaches to estimating text feature importance, compared head-to-head (Fold1)}
\label{tab:s4}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Ablation Method} & \textbf{AUC (text removed)} & \textbf{Apparent Text Contribution} \\
\midrule
Zero-vector (conventional) & 0.573 & +22.9\% \\
Shuffled-feature (fair, proposed) & 0.790 & +1.6\% \\
\midrule
\textbf{Inflation factor} & \textbf{---} & \textbf{14.2$\times$} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Note:} Zero-vector ablation overstates text feature importance by more than an order of magnitude. The problem is straightforward: feeding a batch of all-zero vectors into a batch-normalized layer collapses the running statistics, and the resulting instability gets wrongly chalked up to ``the features were important.'' Shuffling preserves the marginal distribution of each feature while breaking its association with specific genes, which is a much fairer baseline. We caught this late in the project---earlier drafts of this work used the inflated 22.9\% figure---and we recommend checking for this artifact in any GNN with BN layers.

\section*{Appendix A. SLMGAE Head-to-Head Implementation Details}

We built SLMGAE as two separate graph encoder branches---one operating on the SL adjacency, one on the PPI adjacency---each with two message-passing layers, followed by an attention gate that learns how much to trust each view. The decoder is a bilinear operation on the fused embeddings. Input features were 256-dimensional random vectors, matching the original paper.

The only thing that differed between our ``standard'' and ``fair'' runs was which SL pairs went into the adjacency matrix. For the leaked version, we built the SL graph from all positive pairs before splitting---which is how most published implementations work. For the fair version, we held out every pair where both genes belonged to the test fold.

Everything else---learning rate, optimizer, number of epochs, random seed, PPI graph, training splits---was identical between the two runs. The 13.7\% AUC gap between them is purely from the adjacency construction.
