\opening{Dear Editor,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ``\textbf{Complementary Information Channels for Synthetic Lethality Prediction: Augmenting Graph Topology with Protein Language Models and LLM Weak Supervision}'' for consideration for publication in \textit{Briefings in Bioinformatics}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Summary of the Work}

Synthetic lethality (SL) prediction is fundamental to anticancer drug target discovery, yet cold-start generalization---predicting SL for gene pairs unseen during training---remains challenging for all existing methods. We present a three-layer collaborative framework that integrates large language model (LLM) literature mining, GATv2 graph neural networks, and ESM-2 protein language model embeddings. Through rigorous head-to-head comparison, we demonstrate that while PPI topology is the dominant information source for SL prediction, our orthogonal information channels---ESM-2 embeddings (+12.2\% AUC) and LLM weak supervision (+1.6\% AUC)---provide complementary predictive signals.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Key Contributions and Novelty}

Our work makes three distinct contributions to the SL prediction methodology literature:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Identification and quantification of SL adjacency information leakage} in standard multi-view GAE cold-start evaluations: we demonstrate that including test SL pairs in the adjacency matrix artificially inflates CV3 AUC by 13.7\%. We propose fair masking as a required protocol for future benchmark studies, extending the Feng \textit{et al.} (2024, \textit{Nat. Commun.}) benchmark framework.

    \item \textbf{Discovery and quantification of zero-vector ablation bias} in batch-normalized GNNs: we show that the conventional practice of using zero vectors for feature ablation overestimates feature importance by 14$\times$ (apparent +22.9\% vs. true +1.6\%). We recommend shuffled-feature ablation as a new methodological standard.

    \item \textbf{The first demonstration of LLM weak supervision as a literature mining paradigm for SL prediction}, achieving 100\% NER precision at ${<}$\$5 total API cost, and validated through independent DepMap CRISPR screening across 1,208 cell lines (1.73$\times$ enrichment, Fisher's exact $p$=0.041).
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Relevance to \textit{Briefings in Bioinformatics}}

Our manuscript aligns with the journal's scope across multiple dimensions: (i) it presents a novel computational methodology integrating three rapidly converging trends (GNNs, protein language models, and LLM-based literature mining); (ii) it provides two broadly applicable methodological insights for evaluation protocol design in biomedical machine learning; and (iii) it includes comprehensive independent validation through DepMap CRISPR data. The honest, self-critical evaluation style---acknowledging the dominance of graph topology over our complementary signals---reflects the methodological rigor expected by \textit{Briefings in Bioinformatics}.
